# Potential-User Survey Design

## 1. Research Purpose

The purpose of this research is to collect evidence about how potential users participate in online thematic communities, what problems they experience, and what functionality they consider important.

The research is intended to validate or challenge assumptions made during the initial project proposal.

The proposed system is not presented to respondents before their existing experience and preferences are collected. This reduces the risk of leading respondents toward the intended solution.

## 2. Target Participants

The target participants are people with experience using online communities organized around shared interests.

Relevant platforms include Discord, Telegram, Reddit, traditional forums, and similar community platforms.

Participation in the survey is anonymous, and personally identifiable information is not required.

## 3. Research Method

The research uses two complementary methods:

1. an anonymous structured online survey;
2. short semi-structured discussions with representatives of the target user group.

The survey provides quantitative and categorical evidence about user behavior, preferences, and commonly experienced problems.

The discussions provide additional qualitative context about how users interact with existing platforms and why they use particular communication methods.

Detailed cross-participant interpretation is performed separately during the research-analysis stage.

## 4. Survey Questions

### Q1. Community Usage Frequency

**Question:** How often do you use online communities related to your interests?

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Options:**
- Daily
- Several times a week
- Several times a month
- Rarely
- I do not currently use online communities

### Q2. Platforms Used

**Question:** Which platforms do you use for interest-based online communities?

**Type:** Multiple selection

**Options:**
- Discord
- Telegram
- Reddit
- Facebook Groups
- Traditional forums
- Discourse-based communities
- Other

### Q3. Community Activities

**Question:** What do you usually do in online communities?

**Type:** Multiple selection

**Options:**
- Read discussions
- Search for information
- Ask questions
- Create posts or share content
- Comment on discussions
- Chat with other members
- Follow news or updates
- Participate in events or activities
- Other

### Q4. Overall Satisfaction

**Question:** How satisfied are you with the online community platforms you currently use?

**Type:** Linear scale from 1 to 5

- 1 — Very dissatisfied
- 5 — Very satisfied

### Q5. Information Retrieval

**Question:** How easy is it to find useful information or previously discussed topics in the communities you use?

**Type:** Linear scale from 1 to 5

- 1 - Very difficult
- 5 - Very easy

### Q6. Experienced Problems

**Question:** Which problems have you experienced in online communities?

**Type:** Multiple selection

**Options:**
- Important information gets lost in chat messages
- It is difficult to find old discussions
- Too many unrelated messages or notifications
- Discussions become disorganized
- I need to use multiple platforms for the same community
- Moderation is insufficient or inconsistent
- It is difficult to discover relevant communities
- Communication between members is limited
- I have not experienced significant problems
- Other

### Q7. Multiple Platform Usage

**Question:** Do you use multiple platforms for the same interest or community?

**Type:** Multiple choice

**Options:**
- Yes, frequently
- Yes, sometimes
- No

### Q8. Reasons for Multiple Platform Usage

**Question:** If you use multiple platforms for the same interest or community, why?

**Type:** Multiple selection

**Options:**
- One platform is better for organized discussions
- One platform is better for quick conversations
- Different members use different platforms
- Some content is available only on a particular platform
- Different platforms provide different moderation or management features
- Other
- I do not use multiple platforms for the same interest or community

### Q9. Organized Discussions

**Question:** How important is it for an online community to have organized and persistent discussions that can be found later?

**Type:** Linear scale from 1 to 5

- 1 - Not important
- 5 - Very important

### Q10. Quick Communication

**Question:** How important is quick, informal communication with other community members?

**Type:** Linear scale from 1 to 5

- 1 - Not important
- 5 - Very important

### Q11. Feature Importance

**Question:** Which features are most important to you in an online community platform?

**Type:** Multiple selection

**Options:**
- Organized posts and discussions
- Comments and replies
- Real-time or near-real-time chat
- Search
- Community discovery
- Moderation tools
- User roles and permissions
- Notifications
- User profiles
- Other

### Q12. Open Feedback

**Question:** If you could improve one thing about the online communities or platforms you currently use, what would it be?

**Type:** Open-ended response

## 5. Semi-Structured Discussion Questions

The qualitative discussions use four core questions:

1. What are the main problems you experience when using online communities?
2. When you need a discussion or answer that can be found and reread later, what do you normally use? What do you use for quick communication?
3. Do you use multiple platforms for the same interest or community? If yes, why?
4. If you could improve one thing about the online communities you use, what would it be?

Clarifying follow-up questions may be asked when necessary, but participants are not directed toward a preferred answer.

## 6. Evidence Handling

Survey results are reported in aggregated form where appropriate.

Discussion participants are anonymized using identifiers such as P1, P2, P3, and P4.

The research documentation distinguishes between:

- reported evidence;
- interpretations derived from evidence;
- project assumptions that remain unverified.

Negative and contradictory evidence is retained.

## 7. Research Limitations

The research uses convenience sampling and therefore is not intended to statistically represent the entire population of online-community users.

The participant sample may be concentrated around university students and users of particular communication platforms.

The research records self-reported experience rather than directly observed behavior.

These limitations must be considered when interpreting the results.
